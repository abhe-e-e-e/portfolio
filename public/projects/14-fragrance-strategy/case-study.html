14 / Try the scent before choosing the bottle

Independent luxury fragrance strategy exercise, inspired by interest developed through Brandstorm. This is a new portfolio concept, not a reconstruction of earlier competition materials, an endorsed L’Oréal proposal, or a claim of launched sales. Research checked 15 November 2026.

Decision and problem

Explore a discovery-first fragrance offer for buyers who enjoy fragrance but hesitate to choose an expensive full bottle online. The proposed benefit is confident selection after wearing a scent over several days. It is a hypothesis about customer behaviour, not a survey finding.

Start with three 2 ml samples and a guided wear diary, then offer one 50 ml bottle. Avoid a broad collection and unproven personalised formulas: the first experiment should isolate whether sampling changes purchase intent and economics. A fictional working label is unnecessary until the offer is validated.

What the sources establish

L’Oréal reported that fragrances remained dynamic in its Luxe division in 2025. Its overall group results cannot establish the size of a Dubai niche, willingness to buy this concept, or an achievable share. Use that evidence only as category context. L’Oréal 2025 annual results, published 12 February 2026.

Diptyque's UK shop lists a five-piece 7.5 ml eau de parfum discovery set at GBP 139 and a 75 ml Do Son holiday eau de parfum at GBP 170. These are region-specific observed retail references, not UAE quotes, like-for-like comparisons or a currency conversion. The formats show an established discovery-to-bottle architecture. Diptyque UK, accessed 15 November 2026.

Le Labo offers discovery sets alongside its full fragrance collection. This supports the existence of the format among established brands; it does not prove that customers will choose an unknown entrant. Le Labo discovery sets, accessed 15 November 2026.

Positioning and alternatives

Target hypothesis: Dubai-based fragrance buyers who have bought premium fragrance before but want to test unfamiliar scents on skin. Exclude collectors seeking scarcity and gift buyers needing immediate delivery from the initial experiment: those are different jobs with different tradeoffs.

Proposed positioning: a considered three-scent wardrobe with clear wear notes and a deliberate path to one bottle. This is not a claim of superior ingredients, longevity, sustainability or health benefit. Those would require formulation, testing and substantiation. The three briefs could explore fresh citrus/woods, soft floral/musk and warm spice/resin; these are creative directions, not manufactured products or tested formulas.

Pricing and unit economics: assumptions only

Test a discovery kit at AED 60 and a 50 ml bottle at AED 420, with AED 30 credit for a kit buyer. The numbers are proposed test prices, not validated willingness to pay. All values below exclude VAT, returns and fixed development costs, and use fictional costs.

For each kit: 18 product/packaging + 15 fulfilment + 2 transaction fees = 35 variable cost. Kit contribution before acquisition is 25. Assume acquisition costs 45 per kit customer. For a converted bottle: 420 - 30 credit - 110 product/packaging - 20 fulfilment - 12 fees = 248 contribution.

expected_contribution_per_acquired_kit_customer = 25 - CAC + conversion_rate * 248

At CAC 45 and 15% conversion, this is AED 17.20. Break-even conversion at that CAC is (45 - 25) / 248 = 8.06%. At CAC 70 it rises to 18.15%; at 5% conversion and CAC 45, expected contribution is negative AED 7.60. These calculations exclude refunds, samples lost in transit, discount leakage, formula development, minimum order quantities and overhead. They are not a forecast.

Launch sequence and experiment

Phase 1: conduct 15 interviews about the last actual fragrance purchase, avoiding leading questions about the concept. Compare sampling behaviour and abandoned purchases. Phase 2: obtain a qualified manufacturer's feasibility and cost quotations and confirm product, labelling and sale requirements before selling any cosmetic. Phase 3: run a limited prototype test with transparent product status and no invented reviews or scarcity. Phase 4: if viable, test a paid kit with a defined credit expiry and a clear refund policy.

Randomise eligible consenting kit customers between a plain scent note card and a three-day wear diary. Hold price, scent selection, advertising channel and credit constant. Primary metric: net bottle conversion within 30 days, after cancellations/refunds. Secondary metrics: diary completion, time to purchase, support cost and stated confidence. Guardrails: complaints, allergic reactions and returns. Record assignment before exposure and analyse all assigned customers. Do not exclude those who ignore the diary.

Exploratory feasibility batch: 40 customers per arm, explicitly too small to establish a modest conversion lift. If baseline conversion is 15% and the minimum meaningful lift is to 25%, a rough two-sided 5% significance, 80% power calculation requires about 250 customers per arm; confirm the design before spending. At assumed CAC 45 and 500 customers, acquisition alone is AED 22,500. Do not describe an 80-person exploratory batch as a conclusive A/B test.

Decision rules

Continue only if observed contribution is positive after refunds and acquisition, quality complaints are resolved, and demand persists without a large discount. If the diary changes engagement but not purchasing, simplify it. If sampling attracts collectors without bottle purchases, consider a profitable standalone kit or abandon the credit model. If validated manufacturing costs eliminate margin, change price or scope before launch.

Limitations and reproducibility

No interviews, manufactured formula, consumer test, launch or sales have occurred. Published company results are not independent market sizing; competitor formats are reference points, not proof of differentiation. Reproduce scenario values with the displayed expected-contribution formula. The downloadable source ledger records URLs, retrieval dates and the narrow claim supported by each source.

Portfolio writeup

Developed an independent discovery-first fragrance strategy with a clear target hypothesis, public competitor references, a transparent pricing model and a test plan. The model identifies the conversion and acquisition-cost combinations required for positive contribution without presenting assumed demand as a result.
